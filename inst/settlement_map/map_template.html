      About this map

      Each dot is a tower in the census sampling frame, coloured by the draft settlement type it is given. Its shape shows the number of bells: the more bells, the more sides, and the more area, since every shape spans the same width. Rings of 5 or fewer are small circles. Faded shapes are unringable rings: outside the sampling frame, shown so that every tower with bells can be seen. Each is given the type its own place would have, by the same rule, but none affects any other tower's type. Click a dot for the measures behind its type. The types are provisional: see the analysis chapter Choosing a classification.

      Outside cities, towns and villages are set by the density of people within a short walk of the church: people per km² of land, nearer people counting more (the weight halves every 250 m). Inside a continuous built-up area of 50,000 or more, a church is in a centre if it is within 250 m of a high street of District Centre size or larger, or if it stands among three or more towers; otherwise it is in a suburb. Outside the UK, where high streets are not mapped, the buildings round the church must be at least as commercial as its town's as a whole. Every town has at least one centre. A bold outline marks a tower cluster: five or more towers within 1 km, close enough for one band to ring several.
